Where I land: The bull's fundamental case is strong and I largely agree that the balance sheet critique is overstated — JPM's capital position is genuinely fortress-like by banking standards, and the negative operating cash flow is a normal artifact of balance sheet growth for a bank. However, the bear's technical concerns are not trivial. The stock is at a pivot point with no clear catalyst, momentum is flat-to-softening, and the risk/reward at $190.83 is roughly symmetric (upside to $195.45 is ~2.4%, downside to $186 support is ~2.5%). The bull's claim of 1:1.9 risk/reward assumes a breakout to $200 that has no evidence supporting it yet.

This is a genuinely balanced situation: strong fundamentals and a confirmed longer-term uptrend argue against selling, but a stalled technical picture with no catalyst and contracting volume argues against aggressive buying at current levels. The prudent stance is to maintain the position and wait for either a confirmed breakout above $195.45 (which would validate the bull case and justify adding) or a break below $186 (which would validate the bear case and justify trimming). Neither side has decisively won this debate.
