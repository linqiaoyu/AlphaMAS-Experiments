Exhaustion can reverse quickly if new sellers step in.

### Rebuttal #3: The "Negative Operating Cash Flow" is Not a Misunderstanding — It’s a Warning

**Bull’s Claim:** Banks naturally have negative operating cash flow when they grow their balance sheet, and the +$141.2B financing inflow proves strength.

The bull calls this "strong funding access," but I call it **increasing leverage**. A bank that needs to raise $141 billion in a single quarter to fund its operations is not "thriving" — it’s **stretching its balance sheet**. If credit conditions tighten or deposit costs rise, this funding becomes more expensive, squeezing net interest margins.

The **equity-to-assets ratio of 8.2%** sounds solid, but it’s actually **below the 10% threshold** that regulators and stress tests often view as a strong capital buffer for G-SIBs. JPM is leveraged **91.8%**. That’s fine in good times, but it amplifies losses in bad ones.
