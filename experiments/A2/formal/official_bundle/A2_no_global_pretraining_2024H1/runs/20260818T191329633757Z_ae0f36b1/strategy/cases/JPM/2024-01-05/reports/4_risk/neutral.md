That is not riding a leader; that is buying at the most extended point of a move with the worst possible risk/reward.

But the Conservative Analyst is equally problematic in the opposite direction. The insistence that we should not add on a pullback to $148-$152 unless RSI resets below 60 and MACD histogram turns positive again is overly rigid. That would mean waiting for a 10% correction in a stock that is in a confirmed uptrend with price above all key moving averages. The 50 SMA is rising—it was $138.43 on Dec 6 and is now $146.67. If we wait for a pullback to that level, we are waiting for a significant trend disruption, not a normal consolidation. The Conservative Analyst also overstates the risk of the -$47.3 billion operating cash flow. For a bank, operating cash flow is heavily influenced by balance sheet growth—loans originated, securities purchased, deposits deployed. A negative operating cash flow in a rising rate environment is often a sign of aggressive capital deployment, not distress. The Conservative Analyst admits we cannot assess profitability because income statement data is unavailable, yet uses that same data gap to imply the worst-case scenario. That is inconsistent.

The real issue here is that both analysts are treating this as a binary choice: either ride the trend aggressively or protect gains defensively. The balanced approach recognizes that both the trend and the overbought conditions are real, and the correct response is to manage the tension rather than pick a side.

But the RSI at 81.95 is in the 99th percentile, the MACD histogram has collapsed by 87.5%, and price is stretched 19.5% above the 200 SMA. These are also facts. Neither set of facts cancels the other. The trend can remain intact while a pullback occurs. I would suggest a stop closer to $158.50—about 3% below current price—which is below the 10 EMA and gives the position room to breathe while still protecting the bulk of the 60-day gains. For new capital, the trader's plan to wait for a pullback to $148-$152 is reasonable, but I would not require the RSI to reset below 60 as the Conservative Analyst demands. A pullback to the 50 SMA area with RSI in the 50-60 range would be sufficient to establish a tranche, sized at 25-33% of the intended full position.

The Aggressive Analyst's suggestion to add aggressively on a breakout above $166.31 is the most dangerous idea on the table. That would mean buying at the upper Bollinger Band with RSI already at 82. If the breakout fails—which is common after extended moves—we would be buying the exact top. The Conservative Analyst's suggestion to wait for a full RSI reset below 60 before any addition is too restrictive and would likely mean missing the next leg up entirely.

On the data gaps, both analysts are guilty of overinterpreting silence. The Aggressive Analyst treats the absence of news as a positive; the Conservative Analyst treats it as a potential hidden negative. The truth is that the news and sentiment data are simply unavailable due to historical mode limitations. We cannot draw any directional conclusion from that. What we can conclude is that the technical and fundamental evidence we do have points to a high-quality bank in a strong uptrend that is currently overbought. That is not a reason to sell, and it is not a reason to chase. It is a reason to hold what we have, protect gains with a sensible stop, and wait for a better entry to add.

There will always be another opportunity. The goal is not to capture every dollar of upside; it is to compound returns over time without giving back large chunks of gains to avoidable drawdowns.
