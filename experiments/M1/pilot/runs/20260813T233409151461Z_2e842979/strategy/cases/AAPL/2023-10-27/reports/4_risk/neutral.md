You also claim that the absence of news and sentiment data means we are “flying blind” and should therefore reduce risk. That is a reasonable risk-management principle, but you take it to an extreme: you recommend cutting exposure by half and setting a hard stop at $163.00. Those are two different things, and you have no evidence that the latter is occurring.

The fundamental evidence is genuinely bullish: $88.9 billion in operating cash flow, a $335 billion asset base, and aggressive capital returns that signal management confidence. The sentiment and news data are genuinely unavailable—not neutral, not bearish, not bullish, but absent. When you have a bearish technical setup, a bullish fundamental snapshot, and no fresh catalyst in either direction, the correct action is to do nothing. That is not paralysis; that is the recognition that the risk-reward is symmetric at this moment, and acting on either side would be based on incomplete information.

It waits for confirmation—either a technical reclaim or a fresh earnings catalyst—before committing new capital. That is not aggressive, and it is not conservative. It is prudent, and it is the only sustainable strategy given the evidence we actually have.
