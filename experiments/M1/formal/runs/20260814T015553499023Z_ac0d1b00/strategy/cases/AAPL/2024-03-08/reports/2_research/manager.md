That's selling into strength, not accumulation.

The bull is filling that gap with narrative. The $39.9 billion figure is real but it's the seasonally strongest holiday quarter, and we have no year-over-year comparison to establish whether cash generation is growing or decelerating. The market has seen this balance sheet and this cash flow, and it's still selling the stock down 13.71% — that's a signal we shouldn't dismiss.

On the death cross history, the bear's correction is the more honest reading. In both 2015 and 2021, the death cross preceded further 12-24% drawdowns over 5-6 months before any sustained recovery. The bull keeps stopping the clock at the recovery; the bear correctly notes the timeline matters for a near-term decision. The March 2020 example required unprecedented stimulus and is an outlier, not a template.

Where I land: the bull is right that this is a great company with a fortress balance sheet and that the long-term trajectory is likely fine. But the near-term technical evidence is unambiguously bearish, the growth data is unverifiable, and there is no confirmed bottom. The bear's discipline — wait for a close above the 10 EMA, a MACD histogram contraction, or verifiable income statement data — is the more prudent approach for a near-term decision. This isn't a Sell because the fundamental floor is real and the oversold conditions do create genuine mean-reversion potential; but it's not a Buy or Overweight because the technicals and data gaps argue against initiating or adding exposure now. Underweight — trim or avoid adding, wait for confirmation — is the honest verdict.
