That is not “sellers exhausted”; that is buyers lacking urgency. Real accumulation after a shakeout shows up as increasing volume on up-days. We have the opposite. The May 20 high-volume down day followed by a low-volume recovery is the classic signature of institutional distribution: large players exiting into strength, with retail dip-buyers providing the exit liquidity. The Aggressive Analyst is treating the absence of a further decline as proof of strength, but the absence of volume on the recovery is the proof of weakness.

**On the “low-volume recovery is a positive” claim:** This is the most dangerous assertion in the entire argument. The Aggressive Analyst claims low-volume recoveries precede breakouts. Historically, low-volume recoveries after high-volume selloffs precede *continuation of the downtrend* or, at best, a prolonged consolidation. Why? Because it means no new buyers are stepping in. The stock is drifting up on inertia, not demand. If the float were truly “tight” and weak hands were gone, we would see volume expand on the recovery as new capital rushes in. We don’t. We see volume contracting. That tells me the next move is more likely down than up, and the Aggressive Analyst is asking us to hold a position based on hope rather than evidence.

**On the MACD histogram being a “rounding error”:** The Aggressive Analyst dismisses a -0.07 histogram as trivial. But the MACD histogram is the *rate of change* of momentum. It has gone from positive to negative, which is a directional shift, not a rounding error. The MACD line at 2.12 is still positive, yes, but it is declining from a peak of 3.10. The signal line at 2.18 is now above the MACD line. That is a bearish crossover, full stop. The Aggressive Analyst wants to wait for RSI to hit 70 again, but by the time RSI confirms a new high, the MACD will have already confirmed the trend is broken. We do not need to catch the exact top; we need to protect capital. The RSI reset to 58 is not a “green light”; it is a neutral reading that gives no directional edge. It simply tells us the stock is no longer overbought—which is equally consistent with a rollover as with a continuation.

We have a *negative* operating cash flow of -$154 billion in Q1 2024. The Aggressive Analyst waves this off as “balance sheet growth.” That is an interpretation, not a fact. Without the income statement, we cannot verify whether that cash outflow is funding profitable loan growth or whether it is funding deteriorating credit quality. We have no net interest margin, no provision for credit losses, no EPS, no ROE. The Aggressive Analyst is asking us to hold a position on a bank stock without knowing its earnings power. That is not conviction; that is gambling. The balance sheet shows $4.09 trillion in assets and $336.6 billion in equity—fine, but that equity is only 8.2% of assets. The Aggressive Analyst is treating a data vacuum as a bullish signal. That is confirmation bias. We have no idea what macro or sector news is circulating as of May 24, 2024.
