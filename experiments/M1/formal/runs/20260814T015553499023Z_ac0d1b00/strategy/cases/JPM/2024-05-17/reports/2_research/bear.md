
Bear Analyst: # Bear Case for JPMorgan Chase (JPM) — May 17, 2024

**"The trend is your friend" — until it isn't. And right now, JPM's trend is screaming one thing: exhaustion.**

Let me engage directly with the bull's arguments, because I believe they're confusing momentum with prudence, and mistaking a stretched technical condition for institutional strength.

---

## The "Overbought Is Bullish" Fallacy

The bull claims that RSI at 74.99 and price above the upper Bollinger Band are "warnings, not sell signals." Let me challenge that framing with actual data.

**RSI at 74.99 is not just "above 70" — it's at a level that has historically preceded meaningful pullbacks.** The bull conveniently ignores that RSI was at 30.39 just one month ago. That's a swing of nearly 45 points in 30 days. This isn't steady accumulation; this is a **vertical move** that has left the stock vulnerable to mean reversion.

**The Bollinger Band "band-walking" argument cuts both ways.** Yes, strong trends can ride the upper band. But price closing at $195.45 *above* the upper band at $195.14 means the stock is now more than two standard deviations above its 20-day mean. Statistically, that's an extreme. The last time JPM was this extended relative to its bands, it dropped from $186.52 to $174.45 in a single session on April 12 — a **6.5% decline on 31.5 million shares** (nearly 4x normal volume). The bull wants to dismiss that as a "blip," but it demonstrates exactly how quickly this stock can reverse when sentiment shifts.

---

## The "Textbook Bullish Stack" — A Double-Edged Sword

The bull points to price above the 10 EMA, 50 SMA, and 200 SMA as unambiguously bullish. I agree the alignment exists. But let me add context the bull conveniently omits:

**The 200 SMA at $158.07 means price is 23.6% above its long-term average.** That's not just "bullish" — that's **historically stretched**. When JPM has traded this far above its 200 SMA in the past, forward 1-3 month returns have been muted at best, negative at worst. The stock is pricing in perfection, and perfection is a fragile thing.

**The 50 SMA at $184.38 is 6% below current price.** The bull says "you might be waiting months for that pullback." I say: **6% is a normal, healthy correction in any uptrend.** The April 12 drop alone was nearly that magnitude in one day. If you're buying here, you're accepting 6% downside risk just to reach the 50-day average — and that's before any fundamental shock.

---

## The Fundamental "Strength" — Let's Look Closer

The bull touts $4.09 trillion in assets and $336.6 billion in equity as a "fortress balance sheet." Fine. But here's what the bull glosses over:

**The liabilities-to-equity ratio is 11.15x.** That's not strength; that's leverage. JPM operates on a model where every $1 of equity supports $11.15 of liabilities. In a rising rate environment — which the bull celebrates for net interest income — **credit risk and duration risk are rising simultaneously.** The bank's massive securities portfolio faces mark-to-market pressure as yields climb. The bull wants higher-for-longer rates to boost NII, but ignores that the same rates **crush the value of JPM's bond portfolio and increase default risk for borrowers.**

**The negative operating cash flow of -$154.2 billion is not "just an artifact."** The bull dismisses it as "balance-sheet-driven." But let's be precise: JPM **deployed $154 billion more cash into operations than it generated** in Q1. Combined with -$43.4 billion in investing outflows, the bank burned through **$197.6 billion** in the quarter, funded only by +$141.2 billion in financing. That's a **net cash outflow of $56.4 billion**. The bull calls this "deploying capital." I call it **liquidity consumption** — and it's happening at a time when the bank needs maximum flexibility.

---

## The "Wait for a Pullback" Trap — Actually, It's the Only Prudent Play

The bull's most aggressive argument is that waiting for a pullback means "missing dollars while optimizing for pennies." Let me refute that with math:

- Current price: $195.45
- 10 EMA: $190.31 (2.6% below)
- 50 SMA: $184.38 (5.7% below)

If you buy here and the stock corrects to the 50 SMA — a **normal, healthy move** — you're down 5.7%. To recover that, you need a 6.1% rally just to break even. Meanwhile, if you wait for the 50 SMA and the stock continues higher, you miss upside — but you also **avoid the drawdown risk**.

**The asymmetry favors patience.** The bull's "scale in" advice — buy half now, half on a dip — is really an admission that the stock is overextended. If it weren't, why hedge the entry?

---

## The "Price Action Is the News" Argument — A Dangerous Rationalization

The bull says "the absence of negative news is not the same as the absence of positive catalysts." True. But the inverse is also true: **the absence of positive news means there's no fundamental catalyst to sustain this rally.**

Here's what we actually know from the data:
- **Sentiment data: UNAVAILABLE** — We have no read on retail or institutional sentiment.
- **News data: UNAVAILABLE** — We have no confirmation of fundamental drivers.
- **Macro data: UNAVAILABLE** — We cannot assess rate expectations, inflation, or credit conditions.

The bull is asking us to buy a stock trading at **all-time highs, 23.6% above its 200 SMA, with an RSI of 75, above the upper Bollinger Band, on no confirmed news catalyst.** That's not conviction; that's **faith**. And faith is not an investment strategy.

---

## The April 12 Warning — The Bull Ignores It

Let me return to the April 12 drop: JPM fell from $186.52 to $174.45 on **31.5 million shares** — roughly 4x normal volume. That's not a "blip"; that's a **distribution event**. Someone knew something, or the market collectively decided the stock was overvalued at that level.

The stock recovered — I'll grant that. But the recovery has been **V-shaped and vertical**, which is characteristic of short-covering and momentum chasing, not steady institutional accumulation. When the next shock hits — and it will — the same 4x volume reversal could easily repeat.

---

## The Macro Reality the Bull Ignores

The bull celebrates "higher-for-longer rates" as a tailwind for JPM's net interest income. But consider the full picture:

1. **Higher rates → higher borrowing costs for consumers and businesses → rising default risk.** JPM's loan book is massive. Credit deterioration would hit earnings directly.
2. **Higher rates → mark-to-market losses on JPM's securities portfolio.** The bank holds hundreds of billions in bonds. Rising yields = falling bond prices = capital erosion.
3. **Higher rates → slower economic growth → reduced capital markets activity.** Investment banking fees and trading revenue would suffer.

The bull sees only the NII benefit. I see the **full transmission mechanism** — and it's not uniformly positive.

---

## The Verdict

The bull's case rests on:
1. **Momentum indicators that are historically stretched** (RSI 75, above upper Bollinger Band)
2. **A moving average stack that is 23.6% above the long-term trend** — a level that has historically preceded consolidation
3. **A "fortress balance sheet" that is actually 11.15x leveraged** with a **$56.4 billion quarterly cash outflow**
4. **No confirmed news or sentiment catalyst** — just price action the bull interprets as "the market's verdict"

**The bear case is not about being negative for the sake of it. It's about risk-reward.** At $195.45, you're buying at the extreme end of every technical measure available. The downside to the 50 SMA is 5.7%. The downside to the 200 SMA is 19%. The upside from here — without a fundamental catalyst — is speculative.

**JPM is a great company. It's a terrible entry point.**

**Recommendation: DO NOT BUY at current levels. If you hold, tighten stops below the 10 EMA ($190). If you're considering new capital, wait for a pullback to the 50 SMA ($184) or lower. The trend may be your friend — but at these levels, it's a friend who's about to ask for a loan.**
