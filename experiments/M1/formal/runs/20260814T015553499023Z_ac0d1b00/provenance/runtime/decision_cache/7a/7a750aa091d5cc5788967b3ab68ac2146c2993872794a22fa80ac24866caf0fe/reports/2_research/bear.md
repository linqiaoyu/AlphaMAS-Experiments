The bull says this reflects "active balance sheet expansion." I say it reflects **massive cash consumption** that required $141.2 billion in financing activities just to stay afloat. That's not growth — that's **dependency on external funding**. If deposit inflows slow or debt markets tighten, this bank has a liquidity problem on its hands.

### The Leverage Reality

The bull calls a 12.15x leverage ratio "conservative." Let me remind you what happened to banks with similar leverage when stress hit. JPM's liabilities are **$3.75 trillion** — that's 91.8% of assets. This bank is **one credit event away from a capital crisis**. The bull talks about "loss absorption capacity," but $336.6 billion in equity against $4.09 trillion in assets is a **thin cushion** when you're holding trillions in loans, derivatives, and trading positions.

That's textbook **distribution** — large players using the liquidity to exit positions. The bull's "accumulation" narrative is contradicted by the very data he cites.

The bull points to support at $182-183 as a "double bottom." But look at the actual price action:

- **June 12 low: $182.58**

That's not a coiled spring; that's a **descending pattern**.
